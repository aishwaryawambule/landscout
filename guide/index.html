Before you buy land

Most land problems in Nepal come from paying before checking. Do these in order, and don't pay any advance (bainaa) until steps 1 to 5 are clear.

  1. See the original Lalpurja (land ownership certificate), not a photocopy. Note the Kitta number, area and owner's name.
  2. Match the seller's citizenship certificate to the name on the Lalpurja. If someone sells on the owner's behalf, ask for a registered power of attorney (akhtiyarnama).
  3. Get the survey map (Kitta Naksa / trace) from the Napi (Survey) office and match it to the plot on the ground: boundaries, shape and area.
  4. Confirm ownership at the Malpot (Land Revenue) office for that Kitta number. Ask for the latest record.
  5. Check there is no loan, freeze or court case on the land (rokka or encumbrance). Ask at Malpot, and check whether a bank holds the Lalpurja.
  6. Check the road. Make sure the access road appears on the map and is a public road, not a private path that can be closed. Check its width; many municipalities need a minimum width to approve a house plan.
  7. Ask the municipality what you can build: setbacks from the road, river and high-tension lines, and whether the area is residential.
  8. Put the deal in writing with both parties' and witnesses' signatures, and pay through a bank, never in cash.
  9. Register the transfer at Malpot and pay the registration fee and capital-gains tax there. Keep every receipt.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a large purchase, a lawyer or a licensed broker can do these checks with you.
